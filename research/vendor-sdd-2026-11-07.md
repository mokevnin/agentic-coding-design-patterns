# Vendor SDD publications: source review and book comparison

Checked on **2026-11-07**. SDD means Spec-Driven Development. This review prioritizes recent substantive first-party publications over launch announcements: AWS/Kiro, Microsoft/GitHub, Google Research, IBM, and selected practitioner accounts hosted by Tessl. Publication dates come from article bodies unless explicitly qualified. Search crawl dates are not publication dates. This is a focused selection, not a complete census or a claim that each source is its vendor's latest publication.

The initial research pass did not change book chapters or candidate decisions. The comparison below records that screening state; the approved chapter edits are recorded under Implementation follow-up. Vendor reports describe their own practices and products; they do not independently establish that SDD improves every team's delivery. Product instructions and performance claims were not exercised end to end.

### Security requirements enter before planning

Kiro's October 30 article inserts specification hardening between functional requirements and planning. Its Power proposes testable obligations from a curated CWE mapping library, using signals in EARS requirement wording. It covers omissions such as authentication, injection prevention, credential storage and tenant ownership checks. The authors explicitly distinguish this from a runtime boundary or a proof that the generated implementation is secure: code analysis, dependency scanning, testing and human review still apply. Weaknesses not signaled by the requirements can be missed. The transferable practice is to review security obligations while the design can still change, then verify the resulting implementation. The plugin is one implementation of that practice. [Source](https://kiro.dev/blog/spec-it-safe/).

### Intent needs observable evidence

The October 20 Tessl post is Ankit Jain's account of his own team at Aviator, not a Tessl engineering case study. It describes reviewing acceptance criteria, constraints and edge cases before generation, then attaching a verdict and evidence such as screenshots or request traces to each criterion. Recurring PR comments become reusable rules with explicit ownership. The stronger claim that humans can stop reading implementation diffs is the author's proposed operating model; this account does not establish its safety across task types. A criterion can pass while an omitted requirement remains untested. The useful adaptation is traceable acceptance evidence and maintained rules, without automatically removing implementation review. [Source](https://tessl.io/blog/we-stopped-reviewing-code-and-started-verifying-intent).

### Team standards become reusable workflow components

Microsoft's October 7 article describes Spec Kit customization through presets, extensions, bundles and workflows. Presets adapt existing templates and terminology; extensions add capabilities; bundles package versioned setups; workflows connect steps, evidence and human checkpoints. Governed organization catalogs are distinguished from community discovery, where a listing does not mean a security review. The same layering is proposed for constitutions: organization expectations, domain or technology guidance, and team additions. This expands SDD from a feature document into an organizational workflow. It supports reusable standards and repeatable setup, but does not mean every project needs all these layers. [Source](https://developer.microsoft.com/blog/from-spec-first-to-enterprise-ready-extending-github-spec-kit/).

### SDD does not require one artifact sequence

Simon Martinelli's October 7 Tessl-hosted account uses reviewed system use cases and an entity or domain model as durable inputs. A use case includes actors, preconditions, success and alternative flows, postconditions and acceptance criteria. He explicitly skips a separate plan-and-task phase in his described process. For modernization, existing behavior is extracted and reviewed by business stakeholders before generating a replacement; it is not accepted merely because it appears in legacy code. Review depth depends on business risk. This is a practitioner workflow for business applications, not a comparative trial or proof that task decomposition is unnecessary elsewhere. It demonstrates that SDD is broader than one tool's requirements–design–tasks pipeline. [Source](https://tessl.io/blog/spec-driven-development-works-when-specs-are-system-use-cases).

### Shared intent is a team concern

Microsoft Digital's September 3 account says individual coding gains did not automatically improve team productivity. Its response is a living specification shared by product managers, architects, engineers, designers, testers and agents. The stated pillars include synchronization with code and tests, human validation and measurable outcomes. This shifts the discussion from producing a plan for one session to preserving intent through handoffs. It is an internal adoption narrative; claims about speed and consistency should remain attributed, rather than converted into universal productivity promises. [Source](https://www.microsoft.com/insidetrack/blog/engineering-the-frontier-firm-sharing-our-ai-native-approach-to-software-development/).

### Contract extraction before test generation has bounded evidence

Google's paper was submitted on August 17; the [Google Research entry](https://research.google/pubs/grounding-ai-agents-in-contracts-an-empirical-evaluation-of-spec-driven-test-generation/) identifies the 2026 SpecOps venue. The experiment covers 90 historical Google production bugs and compares direct test generation with an agent that first documents preconditions, postconditions and undefined behavior. At five attempts, the authors report a 9.8 percentage point improvement in bug detection and 2.5 points in branch coverage. The specification format marks conditions as tested or untested and links gaps to suggested tests. This supports investigating contract extraction as a testing technique. It does not validate an entire SDD lifecycle, generalize to every model or codebase, or turn an inferred contract into authoritative business intent. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.17177v1).

## Earlier context that prevents misreading the recent posts

Microsoft's [June 10 overview](https://developer.microsoft.com/blog/spec-driven-development-ai-native-engineering/) explicitly includes clarification and validation alongside constitution, specification, planning, tasks and implementation. It recommends starting with a focused pilot, maintaining living artifacts, avoiding premature detail and scaling the process only where it adds value. The article therefore does not support making the full lifecycle mandatory for every change.

Kiro's [May 12 update](https://kiro.dev/blog/faster-smarter-specs/) adds workspace-aware clarification, faster artifact generation for well-understood work, selective regeneration after changes, dependency-aware task execution and requirements analysis for ambiguity or inconsistency. Its reported implementation speedup is anecdotal. Shorter approval flows retain spec artifacts; they do not establish that every problem benefits from less review.

Kiro's [February 18 article](https://kiro.dev/blog/specs-bugfix-and-design-first/) distinguishes requirements-first, design-first and bugfix flows. A bugfix specification records current behavior, expected behavior and behavior to preserve. Design-first starts from an existing architectural decision or prototype and derives requirements from it. The article explicitly rejects the assumption that software can be perfectly specified before building it.

Google's [July 16 Conductor article](https://developers.googleblog.com/evolving-spec-driven-development-conductor-now-supports-antigravity/) moves the workflow from a strict command sequence to a conversational plugin while retaining versioned specifications and plans. It emphasizes carrying context between tools. This is a useful tooling update, but persistent artifacts and portable workflow procedures are already principles in the book.

IBM's [May 19 explainer](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/spec-driven-development) distinguishes spec-first, spec-anchored and spec-as-source approaches without treating them as a compulsory maturity ladder. It also discusses hybrids and warns against spending more on specification refinement than the misunderstanding would cost to fix. Its opening definition includes implementation detail, but its later guidance explicitly warns against prematurely fixing the implementation. The distinction between behavioral requirements, technical constraints and implementation design needs to remain clear when citing it.

The [GitHub SDD tag index](https://github.blog/tag/spec-driven-development/) exposed September 2025 launch and Markdown-programming articles and a December 2025 roundup when checked. The October 2026 Microsoft extensibility article is more useful for current Spec Kit direction than treating those launch posts as fresh news. The [current repository README](https://github.com/github/spec-kit) also offers independent entry points for features, bug fixing and idea assessment; these are not mandatory phases of one workflow. The live [SDD concept page](https://github.com/github/spec-kit/blob/main/docs/concepts/sdd.md) says Spec Kit does not prescribe one post-change persistence model for its artifacts. Those are current documentation observations, not newly dated articles.

JetBrains' [Junie SDD guide](https://blog.jetbrains.com/junie/2025/10/how-to-use-a-spec-driven-approach-for-coding-with-ai/) describes requirements, a reviewed plan, verifiable tasks and scoped patches. Its URL places it in October 2025; the fetched article body did not expose an exact publication date, so it is an older reference, not a verified recent post. It adds little conceptual novelty beyond the core workflow already represented in the book.

## Recommended editorial work

These recommendations guided the subsequent edits to existing chapters. They do not introduce new candidates.

1. **Strengthen requirement review in the SDD chapter.** Extend the implementation and example sections with contradictions, ambiguous boundaries and concrete security obligations before design. For the report-export example, decide whose reports each recipient may access and verify that an unauthorized recipient cannot obtain the link. Keep this distinct from restricting the agent's tool permissions.
2. **Close the loop explicitly.** End the SDD example by connecting agreed acceptance scenarios to actual checks and identifying anything still unverified. Link to the existing verification-loop and reviewer chapters. A successful build or a checked task list alone does not establish conformance. Do not introduce mandatory traceability paperwork for every small change.
3. **Describe the four phases as a common workflow.** The invariant is reviewed intent guiding implementation and validation. Document names, entry points and the need for a separate task artifact can vary. A bugfix example can record the failing case, corrected behavior and neighboring behavior to preserve. An already validated architectural constraint is compatible with the existing exception in `premature-specification`; no reversal of that anti-pattern is needed.
4. **Narrow one generalization in `big-spec-up-front`.** Its explanation that SDD toolkits offer a complete process for every task is too broad given the lighter and adaptive flows now described by vendors. Frame the mistake as applying the whole available process indiscriminately. Keep the prototype and short-spec advice as a starting heuristic, with depth determined by uncertainty and consequences.
5. **Refresh supporting references selectively.** Add recent sources to the SDD overview and, if useful, the resource list. Reuse existing chapters for workflow packaging and rule enforcement. No source in this review justifies a new chapter by itself, wholesale replacement of the current toolkit selection, or treating vendor productivity claims as established facts.

Two tempting changes should be rejected on current evidence: removing implementation review because acceptance evidence exists, and requiring full-code regeneration from specifications. The reviewed workflows differ on both points. Likewise, the Google testing result is evidence for a bounded technique, not a measured productivity gain for the book's complete SDD workflow.

## Implementation follow-up

After user approval, the SDD and Big Spec Up Front chapters were updated in Russian, English and Spanish. The changes add requirement review, explicit acceptance evidence, alternative entry points and proportional specification guidance. The existing Mermaid diagram now shows bugfix and validated-design inputs, an optional path without a separate plan and tasks, and a return when results remain unconfirmed. No new chapter or tool profile was added.

Two independent Claude Opus 5.5 review passes covered repository standards and the agreed scope. The actionable findings were corrected across locales; both follow-up reviews reported no remaining findings. Repository checks and the Honkit build passed, and the rendered diagrams were visually inspected in all three locales.
